SIBO — What it is & what actually works

SIBO (Small Intestinal Bacterial Overgrowth) is when bacteria that normally live in your colon overgrow in your small intestine. It's surprisingly common, often missed, and highly treatable once you know what you're dealing with. Here's the plain-English version.

Am I likely to have it?

SIBO often gets misdiagnosed as IBS. If a bunch of these sound like you — and they don't go away — SIBO is worth ruling out:

  • Bloating that gets worse throughout the day (worst after meals)
  • Gas that started or got dramatically worse in the last 6-24 months
  • Alternating diarrhea and constipation, or one or the other consistently
  • Feeling full after just a few bites
  • Food sensitivities that keep piling up (things you used to tolerate now bother you)
  • Fatigue after meals
  • B12 deficiency without a clear reason

Risk factors that make SIBO more likely: long-term PPI use (Prilosec, Nexium), a stomach bug you never fully recovered from, prior abdominal surgery, hypothyroidism, diabetes, endometriosis, opioid painkillers.

How you actually get diagnosed

The breath test

The standard tool is a lactulose or glucose breath test[2]. You drink a sugar solution and blow into a bag every 15-20 min for 3 hours. Bacteria in your small intestine (that shouldn't be there) ferment the sugar and produce hydrogen and/or methane, which show up on the test. The 2023 diagnostic threshold is ≥10³ CFU/mL, down from the older 10⁵ cutoff — earlier catch, better symptom correlation[3].

Ask a functional-medicine MD, gastroenterologist, or a naturopath about ordering it. Kits like trio-smart (hydrogen + methane + hydrogen sulfide) mail directly to your house — ~$350 out of pocket. Insurance sometimes covers it, sometimes doesn't.

There are three subtypes based on which gas shows up. They matter because they have different symptoms and respond to different treatments:

Type Main symptoms Best treated with
Hydrogen (H₂) Diarrhea, cramping, urgency Rifaximin or elemental diet
Methane (IMO) Constipation, weight gain Rifaximin plus neomycin or metronidazole
Hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) Sulfur-smelling gas, diarrhea, food sensitivities Bismuth protocols + elemental diet

Treatment — what actually works

1. Rifaximin (prescription antibiotic) ~72% success[4]

The most-studied option — a 32-study meta-analysis of 1,331 patients found a pooled eradication rate of 72.9%[4]. Standard dose: 550mg 3x/day for 14 days. Doesn't get absorbed into the bloodstream — stays in the gut, kills the overgrown bacteria, spares the "good" bacteria in the colon better than most antibiotics. For methane-dominant, doctors often add neomycin[7].

Downside: expensive if insurance denies it ($1,200-2,000 out of pocket), and recurrence is common if you don't address the root cause (see below).

2. Elemental diet 80-85% success[1]

The most effective treatment in trials[1], but the hardest to actually do. You drink pre-digested medical nutrition (Physicians Elemental Diet, Kate Farms, or Vivonex) for 14-21 days straight. Nothing else. No food. Bacteria in your small intestine starve because the nutrients get absorbed before reaching them.

Downside: boring, expensive ($400-800/week), hunger, mood dips. Cedars-Sinai released a more-palatable version in 2025 — 83% symptom improvement with 100% completion in the pilot study[5]. Worth asking about if your doctor is up to date.

3. Herbal antimicrobials ~46% success[6]

A middle ground. In a Johns Hopkins retrospective study of 104 patients, herbal antimicrobials matched rifaximin[6]. Typical protocol: berberine, oregano oil, allicin (from garlic), neem, and cinnamon in specific combinations, for 4-6 weeks. Cheaper (~$150-300 total), no prescription needed, and it's gentler on the rest of your gut.

Common protocols: Biotics Research FC Cidal + Dysbiocide, or Metagenics Candibactin AR + BR. Work with someone who knows the dosing — don't wing it.

Why root cause matters (recurrence is the real problem)

Killing the bacteria is only half the fight.

Whatever let the bacteria grow in the first place — usually a slow-moving gut — is still there after treatment. Without addressing it, SIBO comes back in 40-70% of people within a year[8].

Common root causes to address:

  • Slow motility (the migrating motor complex, MMC, isn't sweeping properly). Fixes: prokinetic (low-dose naltrexone, prucalopride, or ginger + artichoke extract at bedtime), 4-5 hour gaps between meals, no snacking.
  • Low stomach acid. Fixes: stop PPIs if you can (with your doctor), betaine HCl with meals, apple cider vinegar before meals.
  • Post-infectious IBS. If you got food poisoning and never felt right after — anti-vinculin antibodies. Ask for the IBS-Smart test.
  • Adhesions / structural issues. Visceral manipulation therapy or a GI workup.
  • Hypothyroidism. Slows the MMC. Check TSH, free T3, free T4.

What to do NOW while you get diagnosed

None of this treats SIBO on its own — but it takes the pressure off your gut and lets you function while you sort out testing/treatment.

  • Space your meals 4-5 hours apart, no snacking. This gives the MMC (your natural cleaning wave) time to sweep bacteria down.
  • Try low-FODMAP for 2-3 weeks as a symptom-management diet. Reduces the fermentable food bacteria feed on. Not a long-term diet — just to buy relief.
  • Ginger + artichoke extract at bedtime (Motility Activator or Iberogast) — a gentle prokinetic. ~$25/bottle.
  • Chew everything thoroughly. Not a joke — poor chewing is a real contributor.
  • Stop eating 3 hours before bed.
  • Manage stress. Chronic stress suppresses the MMC directly. Breathwork or 10 min of walking after meals helps.

When to see a doctor now (not later)

Red flags — don't wait

  • Unintentional weight loss
  • Blood in stool
  • Persistent nausea or vomiting
  • Severe abdominal pain that doesn't come and go
  • Family history of colon cancer, celiac disease, or IBD (Crohn's / ulcerative colitis)
  • Any of the "chronic" symptoms above lasting more than 3 months without a diagnosis
