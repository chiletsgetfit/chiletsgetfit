Shoulder & neck relief routine

A 5–10 minute daily routine for the tightness behind your shoulder blade that creeps up into your shoulder and neck. Tap any video to play. Most people feel a noticeable difference within 1–2 weeks of consistent daily work.

The stretches

01 · The most important one 30–45 sec × 2 each side

Levator scapulae stretch

Sit tall. Turn your head ~45° away from the side that hurts, looking down toward your armpit. Use the opposite hand to gently pull your head down and forward. You should feel it from the side of the neck down to the inside corner of the shoulder blade — that's the exact spot you're targeting.

Don't shrug. Keep the shoulder you're stretching pulled down.

02 30 sec × 2 each side

Upper trap stretch

Sit on your right hand to anchor the right shoulder down. Tilt your left ear toward your left shoulder. Gentle left-hand assist on the right side of your head. Switch sides.

03 30 sec at each of 3 angles, both sides

Doorway pec stretch (3 angles)

Forearm on the doorframe, elbow at shoulder height, step through until you feel a stretch in the front of your chest.

Do three angles: elbow low (~60°) hits pec minor — this is often the hidden culprit pulling your shoulder forward. Elbow at shoulder height (90°) hits the main pec. Elbow high (~120°) hits the upper fibers.

04 30 sec × 2 each side

Cross-body posterior shoulder stretch

Bring one arm across your chest, use the other hand to pull the elbow closer. Keep the shoulder blade pulled back — don't let it roll forward, otherwise you're stretching the wrong muscles.

05 30 sec × 2 each side

Thread the needle

On hands and knees. Slide your right arm under and across your body, letting your right shoulder and ear settle toward the floor. This unwinds the rhomboids and posterior shoulder.

06 10 slow reps

Cat-cow + thoracic rotation

Quadruped. Slow cat-cow for 10 reps. Then put one hand behind your head and rotate that elbow up toward the ceiling 10 times each side.

When your upper back is stiff, your neck and shoulders pick up the slack. Fixing this is half the battle.

07 · active reset 10 reps

Wall slides

Back flat against a wall. Arms in a "W" pressed against the wall. Slide up to a "Y" and back down. Keep the lower back from arching off the wall.

08 · active reset 15–20 reps

Band pull-aparts

Light band, arms straight, pull the band apart at chest height until it touches your sternum. Squeeze the shoulder blades together. Free upper-back insurance.

Training tweaks (the real long-term fix)

Stretching helps. Programming is what actually fixes it.

  • For every horizontal press (bench, push-up), do 2 horizontal pulls (rows). Bias the pulling.
  • Add 2–3 sets of face pulls to every upper-body session — cheap insurance for shoulder/rotator-cuff health.
  • If overhead press aggravates it, swap to landmine press for a few weeks — same pattern, friendlier shoulder position.
  • External rotation work (band or cable) 2x/week, 15 reps, light. Wakes up the rotator cuff.

When to see a physical therapist

Red flags

  • Pain radiates into the arm, especially with numbness, tingling, or weakness in the hand.
  • Sharp pain during normal range of motion — not the stretches themselves, but during daily activity.
  • No improvement after 2–3 weeks of consistent stretching and the programming tweaks above.
