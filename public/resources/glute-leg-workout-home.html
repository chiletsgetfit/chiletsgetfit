Workout · Lower body · Glute focus

At-home glute & leg workout

Everything here needs one bench and a pair of dumbbells. About 45 minutes. Do it twice a week with at least two days between sessions, and aim to add a rep or a little weight every week. The glutes grow from heavy hip extension done with control, so the first three moves get the most effort while you're fresh.

How to run it

Effort
1–3 reps in the tank
Every set should be hard, not a grind to failure. If you could do 5 more, go heavier.
Tempo
3 seconds down, drive up
Control the lowering. Pause where the glute is stretched or squeezed.
Progression
Top of the range → add weight
When every set hits the top rep number, go up 5 lb next session and start the range over.
Rest
90 s big moves · 60 s small
Set a timer. Short rest turns a strength session into cardio.

Warm-up · 5–7 min

One round, no rest. The point is blood flow and waking the glutes up so they do the work in the main lifts instead of your lower back.

W115 reps · 2 sec squeeze at top

Glute bridge

On your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width. Drive through your heels, lift the hips until your body is a straight line from shoulders to knees, squeeze for two seconds, lower slowly. Ribs down, no arching the lower back.

W212 reps · slow

Bodyweight squat

Feet shoulder-width, toes slightly out. Sit down between your heels as deep as you can with your heels flat and chest up. Three seconds down, one second up.

W38 reps each side

Reverse lunge

Step back, lower the back knee toward the floor, then push through the front heel to stand. A slight forward lean of the torso puts more into the glute.

W420 reps · fast pulses

Frog pump

Like a glute bridge but with the soles of your feet together and knees out. Pump the hips up and squeeze hard at the top. This is pure glute activation and should burn by the end.

Main lifts · do these fresh

Rest about 90 seconds between sets. Write down your weights, because next week the goal is one more rep or five more pounds.

01 · The glute builder4 × 10–12 · rest 90 s

Dumbbell hip thrust

Upper back on the edge of the bench, dumbbell across your hips with a towel or pad under it. Feet planted so that your shins are vertical at the top. Tuck your chin, keep your ribs down, and drive through your heels until your hips are fully locked out and your body is flat like a tabletop. Squeeze for one to two seconds, then lower under control.

  • If you feel it in your lower back, your ribs are flaring. Tuck the pelvis and look forward, not at the ceiling.
  • Push the knees slightly out as you drive up.
  • Too easy with your heaviest dumbbell? Pause three seconds at the top, or move to the single-leg version below.
023 × 8–10 each leg · rest 90 s

Bulgarian split squat

Rear foot laces-down on the bench, a dumbbell in each hand (or one goblet-style). Take a long stride and lean your torso slightly forward, which shifts the work from the quad to the glute. Lower until the back knee is just off the floor, then drive up through the whole front foot.

  • Front shin stays mostly vertical. If your front knee shoots far past your toes, step out longer.
  • Hold onto something for balance if you need to. Balance is not the goal here, load is.
  • Do all reps on the weaker leg first, then match that number on the stronger leg.

Swap: reverse lunges with dumbbells if the balance is too much today.

033 × 10–12 · rest 90 s

Goblet squat

Hold one dumbbell vertically against your chest, elbows pointing down. Feet shoulder-width, toes a little out. Sit as deep as you can while your heels stay down and your chest stays tall. Knees track over the toes. Stand by pushing the floor away and squeezing the glutes at the top.

  • Depth builds glutes. Below parallel is where they take over from the quads.
  • Heels lifting? Put a small plate or book under them.
043 × 10–12 · rest 90 s

Dumbbell Romanian deadlift

Dumbbells in front of your thighs, soft knees, shoulders back. Push your hips straight back and let the dumbbells slide down your legs with a flat back until you feel a deep stretch in the hamstrings, usually just below the knee. Then drive the hips forward and squeeze the glutes to stand. The knees barely bend; this is a hinge, not a squat.

  • Keep the dumbbells touching your legs the whole way. If they drift forward, your back takes the load.
  • Stop the descent when your back wants to round. Range of motion grows over weeks.

Swap: single-leg Romanian deadlift (lighter, more glute medius and balance).

Accessories

Lighter weights, strict form, rest 60–90 seconds.

053 × 10 each leg · rest 60–90 s

Dumbbell step-up

Whole foot on the bench (about knee height), dumbbells at your sides. Drive through the heel of the top foot and stand all the way up, squeezing the glute. Lower slowly for three seconds. Do all reps on one leg, then switch.

  • Do not push off the bottom foot. Lift it early and let the top leg do the work.
  • Lean the torso slightly forward over the bench to load the glute more.
  • Taller bench means more glute. Lower the height if your knee caves or you have to bounce.

Swap: lateral step-ups or curtsy lunges to hit the side of the glute.

062–3 × 12–15 each leg · rest 60 s

Single-leg hip thrust

Same setup as the hip thrust, one foot planted, the other knee pulled toward your chest or leg held straight. Keep the hips level as you drive up and squeeze. Bodyweight first; add a dumbbell on the working hip once you can do 15 clean reps.

Finisher · pick one

High reps, short rest, chase the burn. Two sets and you're done.

07A2 × 25 · rest 45 s

Frog pumps

Same as the warm-up, but add a dumbbell on your hips and hold the top of the last rep for ten seconds.

07B2 × 15 · rest 45 s

Dumbbell sumo squat

Wide stance, toes turned out, one dumbbell hanging between your legs. Sit straight down, keeping the chest up and knees pushed out over the toes. Squeeze the glutes and inner thighs to stand.

Why these moves

A 2020 systematic review of muscle-activation studies ranked step-ups, hip thrusts, lunges and split squats among the exercises with the highest gluteus maximus activity, well above most machine work[1]. Head to head, the hip thrust activates the glutes more than the back squat[2], and when the two were trained for nine weeks they grew the glutes by a similar amount[3], which is why this program leads with the thrust and keeps a deep squat in too.

The rep ranges are deliberately moderate. Lighter and heavier loads build similar muscle when sets are taken close to failure[4], so with a limited set of dumbbells the lever you control is effort and steady progression, not chasing a maximal weight.
